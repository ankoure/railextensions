## Alignment

Valley Link is a new regional rail line linking the end of BART's Blue Line in the Tri-Valley with fast-growing communities in the northern San Joaquin Valley. The authority was created by state law in 2017. The full plan is a 42-mile line to a new ACE station at North Lathrop. The current **Phase 1** covers 22 miles from Dublin/Pleasanton to Mountain House. It runs in the I-580 median through the Tri-Valley, then over Altamont Pass on an Alameda County-owned rail corridor and new track beside I-580 on the east slope, ending on new right-of-way in San Joaquin County. Plans call for all-day service in both directions, every 15 minutes at peak, and about 30,000 daily riders by 2040.

In June 2025 the authority split Phase 1 into two 11-mile pieces. **Phase 1A** runs in the I-580 median from a new aerial **Dublin/Pleasanton** station next to the existing BART station, through an **Isabel** station in the median with parking along East Airway Boulevard, to just east of the First Street overcrossing in Livermore. There it branches south out of the median on a flyover and runs about two miles to a new platform beside ACE's **Vasco Road** station, with cross-platform transfers. A maintenance facility would be built at the end of Brisa Street, and Phase 1A would use battery-electric trains with charging at both ends. **Phase 1B** continues from a **Southfront Road** station in Livermore over the pass to a **Mountain House** station on I-205 at Mountain House Parkway. A later phase would add downtown Tracy, River Islands and North Lathrop.

## Updates

### 2026-05 — Environmental work runs a year late
The California Transportation Commission granted a 12-month extension, to June 30, 2027, for the $25M state grant paying for Phase 1A's environmental and project-approval phase. The staff report said the work was 80% complete but had been delayed by the Federal Transit Administration's NEPA re-evaluation, a matching CEQA addendum, and tribal and historic-preservation consultation. Local reporting the same month said Phase 1A construction could begin in 2028.

### 2026-03 — San Joaquin COG programs $10M
The San Joaquin Council of Governments unanimously approved programming $10M in state SB 125 transit funds for Valley Link.

### 2025-11 — Phase 1 cost reported at $4.4B
The Tracy Press reported that the Phase 1 estimate had grown from about $1.9B in December 2022 to about $4.4B because of inflation and required federal contingency. Federal grants might cover $1.5B–$2.2B and local matching funds total about $635M, leaving a gap of roughly $2B. Phase 1B is environmentally cleared and designed but has no construction money.

### 2025-06 — Phase 1 split; battery trains replace hydrogen
On June 11 the board approved a phased delivery strategy, with Phase 1A from Dublin/Pleasanton to Vasco Road and Phase 1B to Mountain House. It also approved considering battery-electric multiple units for Phase 1A instead of the hydrogen trains assumed earlier, citing rising hydrogen costs. The authority targets Phase 1A completion in 2032.

### 2025-05 — Federal environmental approval
The FTA approved the project's Environmental Assessment under NEPA, completing federal environmental review of the 22-mile Phase 1. Design had reached about 30% at the end of 2024.

### 2024-10 — Supplemental EIR certified
The board unanimously certified a Subsequent EIR for the revised Mountain House alignment, which moved the east-slope route alongside I-580. The Modesto Bee had reported a Phase 1 estimate of $1.86B, with $814M in hand.

### 2023 — Mountain House chosen as first-phase terminus
In April 2023 the board adopted Dublin/Pleasanton to Mountain House as the locally preferred alternative for Phase 1, deferring the extension to Tracy and Lathrop. In December the state allocated $25M to complete Phase 1's environmental and project-approval work.

### 2021 — Original 42-mile project approved
The authority approved the Final EIR for the full line to North Lathrop, which was originally to be served by hydrogen-powered trains.
