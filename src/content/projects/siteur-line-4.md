## Alignment

Line 4 of Guadalajara's Mi Tren light rail system runs about 21 km from **Tlajomulco Centro** in Tlajomulco de Zúñiga northeast to **Las Juntas**, at the boundary between Guadalajara and San Pedro Tlaquepaque. It runs beside the right-of-way of the Ferromex freight line. The stations, from south to north, are Tlajomulco Centro, **CUTLAJO**, **Lomas del Sur**, **El Cuervo**, **Concepción del Valle**, **Real del Valle**, **Jalisco 200 Años**, **Acueducto** (also listed as Acueducto Solís), and Las Juntas. Before opening, the state said Acueducto might not be finished in time, and SITEUR described an eight-station line at launch.

At Jalisco 200 Años the line connects with the Mi Macro Periférico bus rapid transit line, and a connection with Mi Macro Calzada at Las Juntas was planned as a second phase. Twelve electric trains, each carrying up to 300 passengers, run the full line in about 38 minutes, every 9 minutes, and five feeder bus routes serve the stations. The state government of Jalisco developed the line as a public-private partnership with Mota-Engil, with federal funding, and the state agency SITEUR operates it.

## Updates

### 2026-07 — About 340,000 trips during the World Cup
From June 6 to 26, 2026, while Guadalajara hosted World Cup matches, Line 4 carried about 340,000 trips. That was well below the older lines; Line 3 carried 3.47 million over the same period.

### 2026-01 — Fares begin; 820,000 riders in the first month
The free introductory period ended on December 31, and a fare of 9.50 pesos took effect on January 1, 2026, with a half-price transfer to feeder and Mi Macro routes for Mi Movilidad card users. SITEUR reported about 350,000 passengers in the first week and more than 820,000 in the first month. Twelve complementary works were still unfinished at opening, and the state said service would continue while they were completed.

### 2025-12 — Line opens
Governor Pablo Lemus opened Line 4 on December 15, 2025, at Tlajomulco Centro station. The state put the investment at MX$9.5B after nearly three years of construction and expected about 106,000 riders a day. Service was free until December 31.

### 2022-10 — Mota-Engil selected as builder
The state chose Portuguese firm Mota-Engil, which had submitted an unsolicited public-private partnership proposal. Mota-Engil would contribute MX$5.795B, about 55% of the cost, and recover MX$21B over 38 years. The state and the federal government, through the national infrastructure fund Fonadin, would each contribute MX$2B. Officials said the line should be finished before 2024.

### 2022-05 — Construction begins
Then-governor Enrique Alfaro launched construction on May 22, 2022, at the old Tlajomulco railway station, about 12 years after he first proposed the line. The plan called for 21.5 km and eight stations at a cost of about MX$9B, down from an earlier MX$13B, with completion expected in March 2024.
