## Alignment

SacRT has proposed extending the Green Line roughly 12.5 miles north from downtown Sacramento through North Natomas to Sacramento International Airport, crossing the American River on a new bridge with elevated structures for much of the route. Estimated at about $2 billion, the extension has no committed funding source. SacRT holds land rights on about two-thirds of the route, which expire at the end of 2028 unless more money is invested to keep them. In 2026 SacRT secured $700,000 to study bus rapid transit on the corridor as a lower-cost interim step; the agency says BRT would not rule out light rail later.

## Updates

### 2026-11 — BRT study paused, then resumed
SacRT paused the Green Line BRT feasibility study in October 2026 over concerns about future local funding, then resumed it after support from corridor stakeholders and elected officials. SacRT's project page now expects the study to start in 2027 and finish in early to mid 2029, with environmental review and design to follow if funding and approvals allow.

### 2026-07 — $700,000 secured for BRT feasibility study
SacRT received a $500,000 Caltrans Strategic Partnerships grant and a $200,000 Measure A Smart Growth grant from the Sacramento Transportation Authority to study a bus rapid transit line from downtown to the airport. The schedule at the time targeted a locally preferred alternative in fall 2027 and a possible BRT launch in 2030. Light rail, estimated at about $2 billion, remains the long-term goal.

### 2025-09 — Cost passes $2 billion; land rights at risk
Abridged (PBS KVIE) reported that the light rail estimate had passed $2 billion, nearly double a decade-old figure, with completion projected anywhere from 2038 to 2050. SacRT holds land rights on about two-thirds of the route that expire at the end of 2028 unless more money is invested, and was weighing four options ranging from full light rail to BRT only (estimated at about $500 million).

### 2025-06 — Advocacy group outlines project status and funding gap
Sacramento Transit Advocates and Riders summarized the extension's early planning status, noting the project cannot proceed to construction without a secured local funding match. (Green Line service has separately been suspended since June 2025 for construction of an infill station at 7th & Railyards — unrelated to the airport extension itself.)
